## Standalone Onboarding

Setup steps:

1. Business type.
2. Work week and pay period.
3. Clients/projects/tasks.
4. Team invite.
5. Approval policy.
6. Rates.
7. Billing/payroll export.
8. Start first week.

Must take under 10 minutes.

## Templates

Templates:

- Agency.
- IT Services.
- Consulting.
- Construction/Field Team.
- Internal Team.
- Professional Services.

Each template preconfigures:

- task/work types,
- approval rules,
- required fields,
- reports,
- rates placeholders,
- reminders.

## Pricing

Suggested packages:

### Starter

Target:

- small teams.

Features:

- manual entry,
- weekly timesheet,
- basic approvals,
- basic reports,
- CSV export.

### Professional

Target:

- agencies/consultancies.

Features:

- timer,
- rates,
- budgets,
- reminders,
- billing queue,
- payroll export,
- audit.

### Business

Target:

- larger service/field teams.

Features:

- client approvals,
- custom fields,
- kiosk/mobile/offline,
- advanced reports,
- integrations,
- API/webhooks.

## Sales Landing Page Promise

First viewport should communicate:

- Track team hours.
- Approve timesheets.
- Bill clients accurately.
- See project profitability.

Avoid:

- generic ERP language,
- "all-in-one business OS" as first pitch,
- employee surveillance framing.

## Sales Objections

Objection: "We use Excel."

Answer:

- Excel cannot automate reminders, approvals, audit, billing export, or profitability.

Objection: "Employees won't fill it."

Answer:

- timer, weekly grid, reminders, and recent tasks make logging fast.

Objection: "We don't bill hourly."

Answer:

- use it for budget control, payroll, utilization, and fixed-fee profitability.

## Acceptance Criteria

- Product can be demoed standalone in under 5 minutes.
- Buyer sees money value immediately.
- Setup checklist gets first project/team/rate configured.
