# HRMS + Payroll competitor evidence refresh

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-24  
**Status:** Dated discovery evidence; not a new normative requirement queue.  
**Buyer lens:** India-first startups and SMBs; globally extensible HR architecture.  
**Related evidence:** [2026-10-22 market benchmark](./2026-10-22-market-benchmark.md), [existing competitor benchmark](./08a-competitor-benchmark-prd.md). Implementation authority remains the existing suite PRDs and release/traceability rules.

## 1. What this research establishes

This is a targeted review of public first-party product pages and help documentation for 23 relevant vendors. It establishes **marketed capability**, sometimes supplemented by a documented workflow. It does not establish tested quality, current customer entitlement, legal compliance, implementation depth, or superiority. No vendor demo, paid account, customer interview, API test, or hands-on payroll run was completed. A missing claim means **unknown**, not absent. A product navigation item establishes advertised scope, not equivalent depth across vendors.

The comparison set is deliberately bounded. It does not cover every HR vendor, integration, country pack, industry variant, or private roadmap. It should expand when real prospect losses, customer requirements, or new country entry justify it. There is no defensible basis here for claiming that a finite PRD guarantees the best product or includes every competitor feature.

**Evidence labels:** M = marketed on an official product page; D = workflow described in official documentation; A = announcement or future/rollout qualification. Every vendor below is M unless a stronger or narrower label is stated. Links are evidence reviewed, not endorsements.

## 6. What to call basic, wow, and future

These are prioritization recommendations for the confirmed SMB buyer, not tested customer demand.

**Basic:** Reliable worker records and lifecycle, usable ESS/manager service, leave/time, configurable India payroll, declarations and expense inputs, statutory evidence, approval/freeze controls, payment reconciliation, reporting, access/audit, imports, and accounting/attendance handoffs. Recruitment, performance, and benefits have broad competitive presence and should appear in the product roadmap, with packaging and implementation dates explicit.

**Wow:** A dramatically faster first correct payroll; one readiness inbox that assigns missing inputs to the right person; explainable changes from prior pay; employee pre-pay verification; complete error recovery after partial bank/provider failure; a manager morning brief that resolves authorized team work; cross-module employee lifecycle workflows with visible evidence; cited, permission-safe AI with reliable action previews. These are outcomes to measure, not uniqueness claims. Several ingredients are already advertised by competitors.

**Future expansion:** Additional validated country engines/providers, EOR and immigration connections, regulated benefits marketplaces, and retirement-trust administration. Advanced scheduling, skills/careers, succession, and headcount scenarios remain P1 complete-suite scope under the existing tier register, even if delivered later for the initial SMB buyer. Global readiness needs multi-entity, currency, locale, effective-date, timezone, and provider seams now; operating additional countries is a separate release decision.

**AI positioning conclusion:** “We also have AI” is not sufficient differentiation. Competitors already market assistants, payroll anomaly checks, pay explanations, contextual analytics, and agents. The opportunity is demonstrably better completion, trust, adoption, implementation speed, and support. Measure that against real HR/payroll/employee tasks; do not compare the presence of an AI badge.

## 7. Evidence still required before a parity or leadership claim

For each important capability, maintain: vendor, product, exact feature, market/segment, country, native/partner/managed delivery, plan/add-on, current/preview/announced state, source URL and reviewed date, demo evidence, StreamlineOS requirement ID, implementation state, and acceptance evidence. Use at least: **marketed**, **documented**, **observed in demo**, **tested**, **unknown**, **not applicable**. Mark **absent** only with explicit authoritative evidence or sufficiently scoped direct verification.

Priority demo questions:

1. Can a 25-person India startup migrate mid-year with prior pay, balances, declarations, and opening liabilities without duplicate statutory deductions?
2. Can an SMB with multiple states, an overnight shift, an unapproved attendance correction, a retroactive increment, and one leaver complete payroll correctly with a visible exception owner?
3. What happens after approval if source data changes? Are inputs frozen, results invalidated, and approvals renewed correctly?
4. Does one failed salary transfer leave the paid employees untouched, preserve idempotency, and allow an authorized retry with reconciliation evidence?
5. Which statutory outputs are exports, which are filed by a service, and which have actual authority acceptance/payment evidence?
6. Can a manager, employee, delegated approver, owner without employee membership, accountant, and HR operator each finish their own tasks without excess navigation or data exposure?
7. Does AI cite the effective policy/record, respect entity/field restrictions, avoid disclosing a coworker's salary, and refuse unsupported conclusions?
8. Can an AI action be previewed, confirmed, audited, retried safely, and stopped by the same controls as its normal UI/API equivalent?
9. How long until first correct payroll, how many manual corrections remain, what support is included, and what is the full cost including implementation, modules, integrations, and statutory services?
10. What works on mobile, with poor connectivity, using assistive technology, and for employees who rarely use software?

These are evaluation scenarios, not completed tests. Acceptance fixtures must be validated by experienced HR/payroll operators and relevant statutory authorities. This report makes no fresh legal interpretation and does not replace the existing India statutory PRDs.

## 8. Navigation implication

Competitor suite breadth should inform domain coverage, not create one permanent sidebar item per feature. Group capabilities by employee and operator jobs; expose detail inside contextual pages, task inboxes, search, and command actions. Separate role audiences and country/module eligibility. For an SMB, importing 80 navigation links is a product defect even if every link represents a legitimate future requirement.

Before sidebar work, reconcile every existing route, permission, deep link, saved view, and workflow entry point. Verify task completion and permission enforcement after any movement. Source review supports this information architecture recommendation; it does not establish that a navigation refactor is harmless without regression evidence.
